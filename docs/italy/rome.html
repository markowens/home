Possible Itinerary

Rome

A free day ahead of Sicily, then three full days based out of Via Giulia.

DAY 0 Ancient Rome Free day, prior to Sicily
Morning

Colosseum + Roman Forum + Palatine Hill on the combined ticket, timed entry booked ahead. Go early — by midday in October it's still warm and the site has zero shade.

Lunch

Walk 10 minutes uphill into Monti, Rome's oldest neighborhood, and eat there instead of anywhere adjacent to the Colosseum — prices right at the exit are inflated for what you get. Ai Tre Scalini is a good, unfussy option.

Afternoon

Wander Monti itself — vintage shops, small ateliers, none of the souvenir-shop density of the center. A good "get a little lost on purpose" stretch.

Optional add: Circus Maximus, a flat green expanse where the chariot races happened — free, especially nice near sunset, five minutes from Monti.

The find most tourists skip

Basilica di San Clemente, a few minutes from the Colosseum. Three buildings stacked on top of each other — a 12th-century church over a 4th-century basilica, over a 1st-century Roman house with an active underground spring. You walk down through 1,700 years of Rome. Small entry fee, rarely crowded, genuinely one of the best things in the city.

Dinner

Stay in Monti — Trattoria Monti or La Taverna dei Fori Imperiali.

Rain / closure backup

Colosseum/Forum are outdoor and can close briefly for weather. Fall back to the Capitoline Museums, right on the same hill, indoor, home to ancient sculpture without the crowds.

Via Giulia, 105

One arrival evening plus three full days, based out of Via Giulia — one of the best streets in Rome to be staying on. Renaissance-era, quiet at night, cobblestoned, lined with antique dealers and artisan workshops, and within a 15-minute walk of almost everything except the Colosseum. You lucked out on location.

DAY I Arrival Afternoon Monday, Oct. 12

Low-key and entirely on foot.

Before you even leave the street

Via Giulia itself has three things most visitors staying elsewhere never notice: the Fontana del Mascherone (a grotesque stone face fountain, mid-street), the ivy-draped arch near Palazzo Farnese that used to connect it to the Farnesina gardens across the river, and Santa Maria dell'Orazione e Morte, a small church with a skull-and-crossbones motif carved into the door — it belonged to a confraternity that buried Rome's unclaimed dead. Ten minutes, sets the tone for the trip.

Wander

Campo de' Fiori (5 min). The morning market will be packed up, but by early evening it turns into an open-air aperitivo scene — locals, not tour groups.

Wine

Il Goccetto, a couple minutes off Via Giulia — one of Rome's oldest wine bars, tiny, wood-paneled, no pretension. Order by the glass and let the guy behind the counter steer you.

Dinner

Trattoria da Sergio alla Grotte, near Campo de' Fiori, family-run since the '60s, no English-menu theatrics, excellent cacio e pepe. Reserve if you can — it's small.

If it's raining, or you just want to collapse

Skip straight to dinner. Nothing above requires good weather except the outdoor wandering, and none of it is booked — zero cost to cutting it short.

DAY II Vatican & Prati Tuesday, Oct. 13
Morning

Vatican Museums + Sistine Chapel + St. Peter's Basilica. Book timed entry directly through the official Vatican Museums site, not a reseller — resellers routinely charge double for the same ticket. Go for the earliest slot you can get; the crowd builds fast, and by 11am the Sistine Chapel is a shuffling crowd, not an experience.

Skip the dome climb unless you specifically want the view — it's 500+ narrow steps, extra cost, and the same vantage is arguably beaten by Gianicolo Hill later in the trip, for free.

Lunch

Pizzarium (Bonci), a short walk from the Vatican. Pizza al taglio, and it's the real deal — Romans line up here too; this isn't a tourist-only spot that happens to be near the Vatican.

Afternoon

Real downtime — don't schedule anything. Either head back to Via Giulia to rest, or drift through Prati, the residential neighborhood behind the Vatican: wide boulevards, real bakeries, no monuments, just a normal Roman afternoon.

Evening

Walk back toward the center via Ponte Sant'Angelo right around sunset — the bridge with the sculpted angels, dome of St. Peter's behind you, river in front. One of the best free views in the city, and almost nobody stops to actually look.

Dinner

Back in the Ghetto or Trastevere (see Day 4), or simply Via Giulia's own neighborhood.

Rain / closure backup

The Vatican Museums are almost entirely indoors, so this day is naturally rain-proof. If the Vatican itself is closed (it happens occasionally for events), swap in the Galleria Doria Pamphilj from Day 3.

DAY III Centro Storico Wednesday, Oct. 14

The wandering day — build this one loose on purpose. Start late, no fixed order.

Late morning

Pantheon — go before 10am if you can. Small entry fee (7 Euros), but it fills with tour groups fast.

Coffee

Skip the cafés directly on Piazza della Rotonda — you're paying for the view. Walk two minutes to Sant'Eustachio Il Caffè or Tazza d'Oro for the same espresso locals actually drink, at local prices.

Then just walk

Via del Governo Vecchio and Via dei Coronari — narrow lanes, antique shops, small galleries, no destination required. Your best shot at stumbling onto something on your own.

Piazza Navona

Go midday rather than evening — same fountains, a fraction of the crowd. Don't eat here; it's the most notorious tourist-price zone in the center.

Trevi Fountain

Either very early (before 8am) or after 10pm. Midday it's a wall of people and phones — not worth it unless you catch it quiet.

Spanish Steps

The steps themselves are mobbed, but Via Margutta just off them is quiet, art-studio-lined, and very photogenic — locals call it Rome's most romantic street.

Gelato

Real gelato sits flat and matte in covered metal tins, not piled into bright mounds — that's a sign of added stabilizers/color. Fatamorgana (a few locations around the center) is consistently the real thing, and less swamped than the famous names.

Dinner

Head to the Jewish Ghetto, ten minutes from Via Giulia — Nonna Betta or Ba'Ghetto for carciofi alla giudia (Roman-Jewish fried artichoke), a dish this neighborhood basically invented.

Rain backup

Galleria Doria Pamphilj, a private art museum still owned by the family. Indoor, stunning, and consistently far less crowded than the Vatican — genuinely doubles as a hidden gem even in good weather.

DAY IV Trastevere, Gianicolo & Testaccio Thursday, Oct. 15
Morning

Cross Ponte Sisto into Trastevere before the crowds arrive — quiet cobbled lanes, laundry strung between buildings, Piazza Santa Maria in Trastevere with its mosaic-covered church.

Climb the Gianicolo

The best panoramic view of Rome, and far less crowded than other viewpoints because most tourists don't realize it's there. Great for photos of the whole skyline and the dome of St. Peter's.

Lunch

Cesare al Casaletto if you don't mind a short bus ride out — genuinely local, few tourists, excellent. If you'd rather stay close, Da Enzo al 29 is well-loved but small; go right at opening or expect a wait.

Afternoon

Testaccio, Rome's old slaughterhouse district turned real, unpolished, food-obsessed neighborhood. Mercato Testaccio has stalls Romans actually eat at, not staged for visitors.

Aventine Hill

The Giardino degli Aranci (Orange Garden) for another free view, then the keyhole at Villa del Priorato di Malta a couple minutes away — look through it and St. Peter's dome appears perfectly framed by a garden hedge. Quick, free, worth the short line if there is one.

Last dinner

The night to spend a bit more, if you want to — Roscioli (book ahead, it's excellent and popular) or loop back to Da Enzo — either an easy walk from Via Giulia.

Rain backup

Centrale Montemartini — an archaeology museum inside a decommissioned early-1900s power plant. Ancient marble statues staged among the original turbines and machinery. Striking, indoor, and almost nobody knows about it.
